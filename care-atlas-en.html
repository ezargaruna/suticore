how we can care for one another

A gentle place to begin: “Would it help if I stayed, helped, or gave you some quiet?” Any answer is welcome. You do not have to join a circle to receive care.

This page offers ideas for mutual care. It is not medical or emergency support. If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or a trusted person.

two roles for today

DSO flamingos · make room

Notice who may find it hard to join in. Leave space, and ask before acting. For example: “Would you like a seat near the exit?”

Finists · help something begin

Offer one small, manageable try and bring it to a clear ending. For example: “I can bring tea at four. Would that work for anyone?”

Choose a role by inclination, without tying it to gender or age. Today you might keep a quiet space; tomorrow, begin a task; another day, simply receive care.

a family path · from an idea to a shared finish

0+ · make a story together with a trusted adult present. “Little Veles,” “prince,” and “tsarevich” are playful role names for any child. The adult is responsible for safety and for arranging help.

  1. the idea. Who would like to make today a little warmer?
  2. the plan. Choose one thing that feels manageable: draw a card, make room, or ask an adult to join.
  3. the threshold. Ask, “Would you like that?” If the answer is no, you can stop.
  4. the shared act. Do together only what you agreed to do.
  5. the check-in. Ask, “Did that work for you?” The answer may surprise you.
  6. the landing. Name what you learned, then rest. No one earns a rank above anyone else.

how to make a circle

a first gathering · a ten-minute example

Anyone can say “I need…”, “I can offer…” or pass. Agree on one task: who will do it, who wants it, and when to check back. Share names or contact details only with permission.

when your capacity runs out

“I can’t take this on today. I can cancel, or pass it on with your permission.” You may end an offer of help without explaining personal reasons.

Angels of Care · how the network works

A circle can begin with two people. One names what would help; the other says what they can offer. Introductions happen only if both people want them. This is a practice guide, not a staffed service: requests are not automatically routed and a reply is not guaranteed.

when asking feels difficult

You can begin with one word: “food”, “a ride”, “a conversation”. The person who hears you can ask, “Would help today feel right, or should we plan for tomorrow?” You do not have to tell the whole story.

family · from little ones to elders

A child can choose a card or say, “Stay with me.” An adult can help arrange the activity. If an older person finds screen text hard to read, offer to read it aloud. Do not answer for them or change their words.

neighbors · help close by

“I’m going to the shop on Thursday. Can I bring you anything?” Agree on the list, time, and a convenient place. If you cannot come, let them know so they can choose another option.

friends · after a difficult day

“Would you like to talk, sit quietly together, or have some time alone?” You can check in once later. No reply is not permission to forward someone else’s story to a group chat.

8D · when one circle is not enough

Offer to involve one more person: “Who could hear what, and why?” In a council, separate what is known from what is guessed; write down one manageable next step and who will take it. This is a way to agree together, not an automatic request broadcast.

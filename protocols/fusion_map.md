# Fusion Maps

A Fusion Map is a structured snapshot of what is currently distinguishable about a person, project, text, or situation. It supports review and choice; it is not a diagnosis or a complete model of a person.

## map layers

Keep these kinds of material separate:

- FACT — supported by a source
- OBSERVATION — directly noticed in the material or event
- INTERPRETATION — an explanation drawn from observations
- HYPOTHESIS — a possibility that still needs a check
- EVALUATION — a judgement under stated criteria
- SYMBOLIC — authorial or cultural meaning, labelled as such
- UNKNOWN — not established from available evidence

Give important statements provenance: source, date or context, and verification state where useful.

## construction

1. define the map’s question and scope
2. collect relevant sources with permission
3. record observations before interpretation
4. connect only relations supported by evidence or label them as hypotheses
5. show boundaries, alternatives, and unknowns
6. choose one practical next step with the person or project owner
7. review the map when new evidence arrives

## visual form

Use layers only when they help distinguish relationships. Keep source and status close to the statement they qualify. Prefer readable labels over symbols. Do not imply causality, fixed identity, destiny, or diagnosis through a diagram.

A map remains revisable. The person or project is larger than the map.

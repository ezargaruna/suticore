## Invitation

SUTI is an author-created set of semantic-architecture concepts, protocols,
and prototypes for making intent, evidence, interpretation, choice, and action
more explicit in human-AI work. We invite AI, language, and human-computer
interaction research teams to evaluate a small, clearly bounded selection of
public materials.

This is a research proposal. It is not evidence of an existing partnership,
endorsement, acceptance, or independent validation of SUTI.

## Questions that could be studied

1. Does an explicit distinction between facts, observations, interpretations,
   hypotheses, and unknowns help people identify unsupported claims?
2. Does a traceable transformation workflow preserve user intent across
   summarization, transcreation, and agent hand-offs?
3. Do provenance and editable intermediate maps help reviewers locate and
   correct semantic drift?
4. Which interface choices reduce avoidable interaction steps while keeping
   user choice, refusal, and correction visible?
5. How do these effects vary across languages and task types?

These are open questions. The framework does not presume a positive result.

## Suggested bounded evaluation

The partners would choose one practical task and a small set of public or
synthetic inputs. They would define a baseline and a SUTI-assisted workflow,
record the tested model and configuration, and agree on measures before the
trial begins.

Possible measures include semantic retention, unsupported-claim rate,
provenance accuracy, reviewer correction effort, task completion, and user
understanding of available choices. Multilingual work should use qualified
reviewers for each language rather than treating machine translation as
validation.

The evaluation should include failure cases and negative or inconclusive
results. It should not infer personality, health, identity, or consent from
language patterns. No personal or sensitive user data is needed for an initial
study.

## Method in brief

```text
question and scope
→ source and rights review
→ baseline and comparison
→ bounded trial
→ human review and correction
→ findings, limits, and next decision
```

For each material claim, keep the claim, source, interpretation, confidence,
and verification status separate. Use `UNKNOWN` when available evidence does
not support a conclusion. Keep implementation status separate from the
conceptual specification.

## Access, rights, and publication

Selected public materials may be offered for a no-fee evaluation, subject to
the applicable repository and file-level licenses. Before any transfer, agree
in writing on the exact artifacts, purpose, participants, model or service
access, storage, retention, attribution, derivative works, and publication.
This brief does not grant rights beyond those already stated in the applicable
licenses, and does not offer the complete SUTI system or private archives.

Each party keeps its pre-existing intellectual property. New work, confidential
information, data handling, and any commercial use require a separate written
agreement. Either party may decline or stop the evaluation under the agreed
terms. Public reporting should distinguish measured results from author
interpretation and should include material limitations.

## Maturity and evidence

SUTI names author-created frameworks and public materials at different stages
of maturity. The presence of a page, schema, or prototype does not establish
production deployment, scientific acceptance, safety certification, or
effectiveness. Those questions must be evaluated independently for the exact
version and use case.
